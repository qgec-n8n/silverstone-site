# Research Handoff Summary

## Load-bearing findings retained

- **One query family, one owner.** Broad “AI agency UK” and “AI automation agency UK” terms remain home/pillar territory. Service pages own specific transactional intent; industry routes own sector combinations; London head terms should be consolidated into a genuine London/core page rather than repeated across every service.
- **Premium restraint.** The final copy uses specificity, trade-offs, controlled technical vocabulary and low-anxiety CTAs. Unsupported “best”, “#1”, “award-winning” and “guaranteed ROI” language is excluded.
- **Problem → system → evidence → process → boundary → CTA.** Each page follows this conversion logic without sharing the same visible template or rhythm.
- **Visible objection handling.** FAQs remain useful HTML content, but no FAQPage rich-result strategy is recommended.
- **Human oversight is operational, not decorative.** Approval gates, escalation owners, blocked topics, exception paths and monitoring appear wherever the service can create material risk.

## Repository findings applied

- Canonical paths and the two live aliases were confirmed on `main`.
- The current route-entry experience generates loader, pill, title, subtitle and button labels from route metadata. This pack supplies deliberate per-page overrides so all seven experiences stop sounding derived from one shared function.
- Existing service modules provide useful conceptual signatures: Conversion Path Lens, Product State Stack, Call Flow Oscilloscope, Front Desk Convergence, Editorial Loom and Process Lattice. Their ideas are retained in copy without making the pages dependent on a visual component.
- Required reserved surfaces were confirmed for two website previews, receptionist chat + ElevenLabs call, and voice call + transcript.
- Approved image assignments were retained in the implementation notes.
- The repository benchmark disclaimer and evidence-framing rules were adopted verbatim.

## Legacy ideas retained selectively

- Clear business-problem framing.
- Discovery before production.
- Approved answer libraries and sensitive-topic exclusions.
- Synthetic/demo labels that avoid presenting prototypes as live client systems.
- Related-service pathways and sector routes.

## Conflicts resolved

- The old route metadata repeats “built around a clear business problem” across all seven H1s. It is replaced because it weakens differentiation and creates near-duplicate search snippets.
- The research proposed `Service` schema as valid schema.org vocabulary while noting that Google offers no dedicated Service rich result. The repository currently lists `Service`, `WebPage` and `BreadcrumbList`; the handoff permits Service as descriptive graph data but prohibits presenting it as a rich-result opportunity.
- The research suggests LocalBusiness markup using the verified London address. Service pages do not duplicate that local entity. Organisation/LocalBusiness data belongs on the home/about/contact entity source, with services linked via `provider` only when the implementation is truthful and consistent.
- Several CSV figures are extremely large and originate from published case data. They are preserved exactly but framed only as external benchmarks, not typical outcomes or Silverstone proof.

## Targeted current verification

Official Google Search Central documentation was rechecked on 30 June 2026. Google’s changelog confirms that FAQ rich results stopped appearing from 7 May 2026 and the FAQ documentation was removed in June 2026. The supported rich-result gallery still does not list a Service result. Google’s people-first guidance continues to reject content written to a preferred word count or produced at scale without added value.

EU Regulation 2024/1689 was rechecked through EUR-Lex. Article 50 requires providers to design AI systems that interact directly with people so users are informed they are interacting with AI unless that is obvious in context. The Regulation generally applies from 2 August 2026. The public copy therefore states a disclosure-by-design principle without presenting Silverstone as legal counsel or making a universal jurisdictional guarantee.

## Competitor principles used without copying wording

- Audit-first entry for uncertain buyers.
- Live or reserved demos close to the relevant service explanation.
- Human escalation as a premium reliability feature.
- Named process and technical boundaries rather than tool-name inflation.
- Calm product-studio language for web/app pages.
- Category definition around content operations, not commodity AI writing.

## Research recommendations deliberately not followed

- No self-authored “best agency” article is included in the initial Insights plan; comparison content should be genuinely useful, transparently governed and published only when Silverstone can assess competitors fairly.
- No pricing bands from competitor research are inserted into service copy because repository pricing and commercial terms must remain the source of truth.
- No provider-reported latency, ROI or savings figure from competitor pages is used as Silverstone proof.
