# Silverstone AI live conversion audit v1

Completed: 2026-06-22  
Authority status: independent external advisory audit; current live pages are primary for visible content, while A-01 remains authoritative for repository evidence.

This independent external audit **accompanies rather than replaces** the Prompt A-01 repository/live audit. Prompt A-01 remains the repository baseline. This package adds a current, conversion-led and standards-led reading of the public site. **C-01 and H-01 are downstream consumers** of these findings.

## Evidence labels

- **Verified observation** — directly observed on the current live website during this audit.
- **Repository evidence** — directly supported by the uploaded Prompt A-01 pack.
- **Inference** — an analytical conclusion drawn from cited observations; it is not a measured outcome.
- **Recommendation** — a proposed change; it is not presented as a current fact.
- **Creative proposal** — an optional concept requiring validation before adoption.

## Executive assessment

### 1. Positioning and first-screen comprehension

**Verified observation:** The home page identifies the audience as UK small businesses, promises help with calls, bookings and lead follow-up, and presents “Book Your Free Audit” and “Explore Services” as the two principal actions. [L-HOME]

**Verified observation:** The home-page H1 repeats the phrase “Silverstone AI for UK Small Businesses” within the same heading. [L-HOME]

**Inference:** The audience and service category are legible, but the repeated heading and broad three-problem promise dilute the first-screen hierarchy. [L-HOME]

**Recommendation:** Replace the repeated H1 with one buyer-specific outcome statement, one concrete mechanism statement and one primary CTA. Keep the secondary CTA visually subordinate.

**Verified observation:** The site serves nine distinct industries from shared navigation and from the home and services pages. [L-HOME; L-SERVICES; L-DENTISTS; L-ECOMMERCE; L-ESTATE; L-FITNESS; L-GYMS; L-HOSPITALITY; L-PHYSIO; L-SALONS; L-TRADES]

**Inference:** Broad industry coverage increases relevance for niche searches but makes the parent brand proposition feel less specialised than each industry page. [L-HOME; L-SERVICES; L-DENTISTS; L-TRADES]

**Recommendation:** Make the parent proposition problem-led—missed enquiries, booking leakage and follow-up delay—while using industry pages as the specialisation proof.

### 2. Conversion paths

**Verified observation:** The principal journey is repeated consistently: industry or service education → pricing or booking → a 30-minute free audit; contact is the main fallback. [L-SERVICES; L-PRICING; L-BOOK; L-CONTACT; L-DENTISTS; L-ESTATE; L-TRADES]

**Verified observation:** The booking page explains what happens during the call, states that the visitor may compare providers, describes the call as no-pressure, and offers contact as a fallback. [L-BOOK]

**Inference:** The booking page handles commitment anxiety better than the home page because it reduces ambiguity around the call. [L-HOME; L-BOOK]

**Recommendation:** Reuse the booking page’s “what you leave with” and “no-pressure” language immediately beside high-intent CTAs on home, services, pricing and industry pages.

**Verified observation:** Most high-intent pages rely on “Book Free Audit” as the dominant next step, while lower-commitment alternatives are mainly blog reading or the contact form. [L-HOME; L-SERVICES; L-PRICING; L-DENTISTS; L-ECOMMERCE; L-ESTATE; L-FITNESS; L-GYMS; L-HOSPITALITY; L-PHYSIO; L-SALONS; L-TRADES]

**Inference:** Visitors who are interested but not ready for a call have no strongly productised intermediate conversion. [L-HOME; L-SERVICES; L-PRICING]

**Recommendation:** Add one low-friction action such as a one-page “automation leak checklist”, a short self-assessment or an email-ready requirements brief.

### 3. Proof, trust and risk reversal

**Verified observation:** Home and every industry page present precise efficiency, conversion, revenue, no-show or time-saving figures without visible source attribution beside the claim. [L-HOME; L-DENTISTS; L-ECOMMERCE; L-ESTATE; L-FITNESS; L-GYMS; L-HOSPITALITY; L-PHYSIO; L-SALONS; L-TRADES]

**Verified observation:** The services page says “No invented guarantees” while the wider site includes quantified outcome claims that are not substantiated on-page. [L-SERVICES; L-HOME; L-DENTISTS; L-ESTATE; L-TRADES]

**Inference:** The mismatch creates avoidable trust friction even where the underlying figures may be defensible. [L-SERVICES; L-HOME; L-DENTISTS; L-ESTATE; L-TRADES]

**Recommendation:** Introduce a claim substantiation ledger. For each number, record source, sample, date, methodology, applicability and approved wording; otherwise remove the number or relabel it as an illustrative scenario.

**Verified observation:** The about page describes a London automation studio and states values, but it does not present named leadership, verified client logos, signed testimonials, attributable case studies or independent credentials. [L-ABOUT]

**Inference:** Buyers are asked to trust process language without enough identity or delivery evidence for a high-consideration technical service. [L-ABOUT; L-PRICING; L-BOOK]

**Recommendation:** Add an evidence centre with named accountability, verifiable case studies, delivery principles, security/data-handling summary, partner credentials where legitimate, and clear limitations.

**Verified observation:** Industry pages repeatedly address objections including integration with existing tools, human handoff, brand tone, transparent scope, data protection and avoidance of long contracts. [L-DENTISTS; L-ECOMMERCE; L-ESTATE; L-FITNESS; L-GYMS; L-HOSPITALITY; L-PHYSIO; L-SALONS; L-TRADES]

**Inference:** Objection coverage is a conversion strength, but the repeated assertions would be stronger if tied to evidence, process artefacts or contractual commitments. [L-DENTISTS; L-ECOMMERCE; L-ESTATE; L-TRADES]

**Recommendation:** Convert generic trust statements into inspectable proof: a sample scope, change-control policy, handoff diagram, data-processing summary and redacted delivery timeline.

### 4. Pricing and commercial clarity

**Verified observation:** The pricing page frames packages around setup and monthly costs, includes FAQs and directs visitors to booking. [L-PRICING]

**Verified observation:** The page states that most focused systems go live within one to two weeks and uses “prove ROI” and “fastest payback” framing without visible supporting data or qualification. [L-PRICING]

**Inference:** The page reduces uncertainty about commercial structure but introduces credibility risk by combining concrete delivery and return language with limited proof. [L-PRICING]

**Recommendation:** Present price ranges with explicit inclusions, exclusions, dependencies, typical—not promised—delivery windows and a worked illustrative cost model with assumptions.

**Repository evidence:** Prompt A-01 observed that the live pricing experience used a client-rendered pricing widget with two roots and ten cards. [A01-AUDIT; A01-DIFF]

**Inference:** Important commercial information should not depend solely on client-side execution or an interactive control for discoverability and accessibility. [A01-AUDIT; L-PRICING]

**Recommendation:** Ensure every package’s essential name, audience, inclusions, starting price or “custom” status and CTA are present in server-delivered HTML, with the widget as enhancement.

### 5. Copy density and decision support

**Verified observation:** The services and industry pages use long, repeated structures: problem, solution pack, metrics, outcomes, process, objections, FAQs, related articles and CTA. [L-SERVICES; L-DENTISTS; L-ECOMMERCE; L-ESTATE; L-FITNESS; L-GYMS; L-HOSPITALITY; L-PHYSIO; L-SALONS; L-TRADES]

**Inference:** Consistency helps orientation, but repeated section patterns and long copy make differentiation harder to perceive. [L-DENTISTS; L-ECOMMERCE; L-ESTATE; L-TRADES]

**Recommendation:** Keep one shared decision architecture but make each industry page substantively distinct through actual workflow maps, system dependencies, risks, qualifying criteria and evidence.

**Verified observation:** The blog hub exposes the full article library in a long card sequence with recurring excerpt formulas. [L-BLOG]

**Inference:** The hub supports discovery but makes it difficult for a visitor to select an article by buying stage or operational problem. [L-BLOG]

**Recommendation:** Reorganise the hub around problem clusters and buying stage, then surface a smaller “start here” set before the full archive.

## Recommended conversion sequence

1. **P0/P1:** remove or substantiate quantified proof; resolve the “no invented guarantees” contradiction.
2. **P1:** rebuild the home first screen around one outcome, one mechanism and one primary action.
3. **P1:** add named accountability and verifiable delivery evidence.
4. **P1:** make pricing essentials available without client-side interaction and qualify delivery/ROI language.
5. **P2:** create a low-commitment conversion step and problem-led content navigation.
6. **P2:** make industry pages operationally distinct rather than primarily copy variants.

## Creative proposals

**Creative proposal:** “Find your revenue leak” selector—three questions route visitors to missed-call, booking/no-show or follow-up pathways, then show one relevant industry workflow and CTA.

**Creative proposal:** “Proof before promise” case-study format—baseline, intervention, timeframe, sample size, result, limitations and client approval status are visible in one compact card.

**Creative proposal:** “Automation fit brief”—a downloadable one-page scope template that lets a prospect document tools, volume, failure points, handoff rules and desired outcomes before booking.

## Limitations

**Verified observation:** This was a read-only public-site audit; no form was submitted and no booking was created. [L-BOOK; L-CONTACT]

**Repository evidence:** Prompt A-01 did not access authenticated analytics, Search Console, Netlify, DNS, Resend or Calendly settings. [A01-MANIFEST; A01-AUDIT]

**Inference:** Rankings, traffic, conversion rate, delivery rate, Core Web Vitals and commercial outcomes are not established by this audit. [A01-MANIFEST; A01-AUDIT]
