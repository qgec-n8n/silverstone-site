Where friction appears

Most agencies do not lose enquiries to a lack of effort. They lose them because the routes are tangled and the same lead is chased twice or not at all.

01

Three inboxes, one buyer

Portal leads, web forms and calls land in different places, so the same person is contacted twice — or missed entirely.

02

Viewings booked by phone tag

A simple viewing takes four messages to arrange because no slot is offered at the point of interest.

03

Vendor updates feel automated

Owners want a person; a generic auto-reply at the wrong moment reads as neglect.

Property-enquiry switchboard

Choose an incoming request. The switchboard sorts it into a lane and prepares a summary for the branch — or routes a vendor/landlord update straight to a person.

    What this demonstrates — and what it does not

    Demonstrates

    • Sorting an intake into the right lane
    • Preparing a summary for a person to confirm
    • Routing the sensitive case to a human boundary

    Does not

    • Confirm, book or decide anything automatically
    • Use real customer data or live integrations
    • Cross the human-in-control boundary

    Static fallback: an intake is captured and sorted into a lane; routine requests reach a prepared summary for a person to confirm, and the sensitive request is routed to the branch team.
