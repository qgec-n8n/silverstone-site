Where websites lose clarity

Most sites do not fail on taste. They fail because the path forks too early and the visitor has to do the sorting.

01

Too many equal choices

Five calls-to-action of the same weight mean none of them is the path. The eye stalls.

02

Message after the fold

The one sentence that explains the offer arrives three scrolls late, behind decoration.

03

Proof with no context

Logos and figures sit in a separate band, far from the decision they are meant to support.

    What this demonstrates — and what it does not

    Demonstrates

    • How layout order changes the route a visitor reads
    • Placing proof beside the action, not in a separate band
    • A single dominant call-to-action per page

    Does not

    • Predict conversion rate, ranking or revenue
    • Use real visitor data or any live integration
    • Replace research with your actual audience

    Static fallback: the recommended order is clarity banner → primary action → proof in context → trimmed wayfinding → reassurance → repeated action.
