    Design anatomy

    How a flow is built

    Disclosure

    The caller is told, up front, that they are speaking to an automated assistant.

    Intent

    Speech becomes a structured intent the flow can reason about.

    Capture

    Only the minimum details needed for the action are collected.

    Approved-rules check

    The request is matched against rules you signed off — deterministic, not guessed.

    Action or handoff

    The call ends at a clear action or a clean handoff to a named person.

    ◆ The handoff is decided by your approved rules, not by how confident the model sounds. A person owns every escalation.
    Boundaries

    What a voice agent will not do

    • It always discloses that it is automated
    • It captures only what consent and the task require
    • It routes emergencies and sensitive situations to a person immediately
    No human-equivalence claim. A voice agent handles defined, approved call flows. It is not a person, does not give regulated advice, and does not handle emergencies. Consent and privacy rules are designed in, not bolted on.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    Will callers know it's automated?

    Yes. Disclosure is the first step of every flow. We never impersonate a person or imply human equivalence.

    What decides when a person takes over?

    Your approved rules — not a confidence score. Sensitive, urgent or out-of-scope calls route to a named owner immediately.

    Can it handle emergencies?

    No. Emergencies and regulated advice are explicitly out of scope and are routed to the right human or service.
