How we work

From scope to release, with a boundary at every gate.

One predictable path: understand the current state, agree the boundary, prototype, test, release and review. Each station names an owner, the evidence required and the criterion to move on.

The rail

Six stations, one track

A horizontal rail on desktop becomes a vertical sequence on smaller screens. Nothing is pinned or scroll-hijacked — it is a plain, readable list of stations.

Current state

We map the repeated problem, who it affects and what evidence exists today. Owner: you and us together. Exit: a written problem statement.

Boundary

We agree what software will and will not do, and where a person stays in control. Owner: Silverstone. Exit: an explicit boundary note.

Prototype

A small, honest build of the smallest useful flow. Owner: Silverstone. Exit: a working prototype you can try.

Test

We check behaviour across devices, keyboard, reduced motion and the exception paths. Owner: Silverstone. Exit: a test record.

Release

We ship with monitoring and a documented rollback. Owner: Silverstone, with your sign-off. Exit: a live, supported change.

Review

We look at what happened, decide what to keep and agree the next smallest step. Owner: shared. Exit: a review note and a decision.

Ownership

What you provide, what we own

You provide

  • The real problem and how often it happens
  • Approved answers, content and any brand assets
  • A decision-maker who can sign off the boundary
  • Access to the systems in scope, when agreed

Silverstone owns

  • The design, build and test of each agreed step
  • Clear documentation of decisions and boundaries
  • Monitoring, retries and a documented rollback
  • Honest reporting — including when something is not working
Change & exception

Designed for the unexpected

Every workflow we build has a planned exception path. When something falls outside the agreed rules, it routes to a named person rather than guessing. Changes are versioned, reviewed and reversible.

● A person stays in control of any decision the rules do not clearly cover. Software escalates; it does not improvise.
Evidence

What a prototype must show before release

01

It works by keyboard

Every action completes without a pointer, with visible focus throughout.

02

It survives reduced motion

All information is present with animation switched off — nothing is hidden behind movement.

03

The exception is visible

The handoff to a person is shown as text and as a clear state, not buried.

After release

Support & monitoring states

Watching

Health checks and alerts so a quiet failure does not become a loud one.

Responding

A named owner for each queue and a documented path back to a safe state.

Improving

Scheduled reviews that decide the next smallest useful change.

Questions

Common questions about the process

How long does a first step take?

It depends on the smallest useful flow we agree. We scope to the first release, not an open-ended list, so the first step stays small and honest.

Do we have to automate everything at once?

No. We start with one repeated problem, prove the boundary, then decide together what — if anything — to do next.

What if the prototype shows it will not work?

Then we say so. The review station exists to make that decision openly, before more is built.
